# 📖 AI Glossary: Understanding the Terminology

## A

### AI (Artificial Intelligence)
Computer systems that can perform tasks that normally require human intelligence, such as understanding language, recognizing patterns, and making decisions.

### Algorithm
A set of rules or instructions that a computer follows to solve a problem or perform a task.

### API (Application Programming Interface)
A way for different software applications to communicate with each other. In AI, APIs allow you to send requests to AI models and receive responses.

---

## B

### Bias
When an AI system produces results that are systematically prejudiced due to biased training data or flawed design.

---

## C

### Chatbot
A computer program that simulates human conversation through text or voice interactions.

### Context Window
The amount of text (measured in tokens) that an AI model can consider at once. If your prompt + the AI's response exceeds this limit, the model will "forget" earlier parts of the conversation.

---

## D

### Dataset
A collection of data used to train AI models. The quality and diversity of the dataset greatly affects the model's performance.

### Deep Learning
A type of machine learning that uses neural networks with many layers to model complex patterns in data.

---

## F

### Fine-tuning
The process of taking a pre-trained AI model and training it further on a specific task or dataset to improve its performance on that task.

---

## G

### Generative AI
AI systems that can create new content, such as text, images, music, or code, rather than just analyzing existing content.

### GPT (Generative Pre-trained Transformer)
A type of AI model architecture developed by OpenAI that is particularly good at understanding and generating human-like text.

---

## H

### Hallucination
When an AI model generates information that is factually incorrect but presented as if it were true. This is a known limitation of current AI systems.

---

## L

### Large Language Model (LLM)
An AI model trained on vast amounts of text data that can understand and generate human-like text. Examples include GPT-4, Claude 3, and Gemini.

### Learning Rate
A hyperparameter that determines how much the model's parameters are updated during training. A higher learning rate means faster learning but potentially less stable results.

---

## M

### Machine Learning
A type of AI where computer systems learn from data rather than following explicit instructions. The system identifies patterns in the data and uses them to make predictions or decisions.

### Model
In AI, a model is a trained system that can perform specific tasks. For example, a text generation model can write human-like text.

---

## N

### Natural Language Processing (NLP)
A field of AI focused on the interaction between computers and human language. It includes tasks like understanding text, generating text, and translating between languages.

### Neural Network
A type of machine learning model inspired by the structure of the human brain. It consists of interconnected layers of artificial neurons that process information.

---

## P

### Parameter
A value that the AI model learns during training. Models with more parameters can generally perform more complex tasks but require more computational resources.

### Prompt
The input or question that you give to an AI model to generate a response. The quality of the prompt greatly affects the quality of the response.

### Prompt Engineering
The practice of designing and refining prompts to get the best possible results from AI models.

---

## R

### Reinforcement Learning
A type of machine learning where an AI system learns by receiving rewards or penalties based on its actions, rather than from labeled data.

---

## T

### Token
A unit of text that AI models process. Tokens can be words, parts of words, or punctuation marks. Most AI models have a limit on the number of tokens they can process at once (context window).

### Training
The process of teaching an AI model to perform a task by showing it many examples of input-output pairs.

### Transfer Learning
A technique where a model trained on one task is used as the starting point for training on a different but related task. This can save time and improve performance.

---

## V

### Vision Model
An AI model that can understand and generate images. Examples include DALL-E, Stable Diffusion, and Midjourney.
