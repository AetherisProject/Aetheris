# ❓ AI FAQ: Frequently Asked Questions

## 🤖 General AI Questions

### What is AI?
AI, or Artificial Intelligence, is computer software that can perform tasks that normally require human intelligence, such as understanding language, recognizing patterns, and making decisions.

### How does AI work?
AI works by analyzing vast amounts of data to identify patterns and relationships. It then uses these patterns to generate responses, make predictions, or perform other tasks.

### Is AI really intelligent?
AI simulates intelligence but doesn't truly understand or think like humans. It's more like a very sophisticated pattern-matching system.

### Can AI think and feel?
No, AI doesn't have consciousness, thoughts, or feelings. It processes information and generates responses based on patterns it has learned.

## 💡 Using AI

### How do I get started with AI?
Start by trying simple tasks like writing text, answering questions, or generating ideas. Use tools like Aether to experiment with different AI capabilities.

### Which AI model should I use?
Different models have different strengths:
- **GPT-4**: Great for general tasks, writing, and complex reasoning
- **Claude 3**: Excellent for coding, analysis, and detailed responses
- **Gemini**: Good for creative tasks and multi-modal capabilities
- **LLama**: Open-source option for various tasks

For most beginners, start with GPT-4o-mini or Claude 3 Haiku - they're fast, capable, and cost-effective.

### How do I write a good prompt?
A good prompt is:
- **Specific**: Clearly state what you want
- **Detailed**: Provide relevant context and details
- **Structured**: Use clear formatting and organization
- **Appropriate**: Match the complexity to the model's capabilities

See our [Prompt Engineering Guide](prompt-engineering.md) for more tips.

### Can AI make mistakes?
Yes, AI can make mistakes, including:
- **Hallucinations**: Making up facts that sound real but aren't
- **Logical errors**: Making reasoning mistakes that humans wouldn't
- **Bias**: Reflecting biases in its training data
- **Outdated information**: Not knowing about recent events

Always verify important information from reliable sources.

### How do I know if AI is giving me accurate information?
- **Check sources**: Verify facts with reliable sources
- **Cross-reference**: Compare with other information
- **Use common sense**: If something seems off, it probably is
- **Ask for sources**: Request that AI cite its sources

## 🛡️ Safety & Ethics

### Is AI safe to use?
Yes, AI is generally safe when used responsibly. However, you should:
- Be aware of AI's limitations
- Verify important information
- Use AI ethically and responsibly
- Protect your privacy

### Can AI be used for harmful purposes?
Unfortunately, yes. AI can be used to:
- Spread misinformation
- Create deepfake content
- Impersonate others
- Automate spam or scams

This is why it's important to use AI responsibly and ethically.

### How do I use AI ethically?
- **Be honest**: Don't use AI to deceive others
- **Give credit**: Acknowledge when you've used AI
- **Respect privacy**: Don't use AI to process personal data without consent
- **Avoid harm**: Don't use AI to harm others or society
- **Follow laws**: Comply with all applicable laws and regulations

## 🔧 Technical Questions

### What's the difference between AI models?
Different models have different:
- **Capabilities**: What they can do
- **Strengths**: What they're best at
- **Weaknesses**: What they struggle with
- **Costs**: How much they cost to use
- **Speed**: How fast they respond

### What is a token?
A token is a unit of text that AI models process. It can be a word, part of a word, or a punctuation mark. Most AI models have a limit on the number of tokens they can process at once.

### What is a context window?
The context window is the amount of text (in tokens) that an AI model can consider at once. If your conversation exceeds this limit, the model will "forget" earlier parts of the conversation.

### What is fine-tuning?
Fine-tuning is the process of taking a pre-trained AI model and training it further on a specific task or dataset to improve its performance on that task.
